# What the files are

Every kind of thing you make in Graphite is a file type that other apps already know.

## Notes are Markdown

A note is a plain text file ending in `.md`. Markdown is a way of writing formatting with ordinary characters, so the text stays readable even without Graphite:

```markdown
# Tomato soup

Serves **four**. Ready in 40 minutes.

- [ ] Buy basil
- [x] Roast the tomatoes
```

Graphite shows it formatted as you type: the heading big, the bold word bold, the boxes as boxes you can tick. Open the same file in TextEdit or Notes and you see the characters above. [[Making notes look good]] shows everything Markdown can do.

## Notebooks and slides are PDF

A handwritten notebook is a PDF. Its pages carry their paper (blank, dotted, squared, ruled, Cornell and more) and your ink is saved as standard PDF annotations, so Preview, Acrobat and Obsidian show what you wrote. Slides and papers you write on stay PDFs too. See [[Notebooks and paper]] and [[Writing on slides]].

## Drawings are pictures

A drawing inside a note is a picture file, PNG by default (or PDF or SVG, in Settings › Pencil Drawings › **Save Drawings As**). Graphite keeps your strokes inside the file, so you can open it again and change it stroke by stroke; every other app simply sees a picture. See [[Drawings in notes]].

## Recordings are audio or video

A lecture recording is an `.m4a` audio file, or an `.mp4` video, saved with the note's attachments. See [[Recording a lecture]].

## Settings live in a hidden folder

A vault may have a folder called `.obsidian`. It holds the vault's settings: its theme, its templates folder, its keyboard shortcuts. Graphite and Obsidian share it. It is hidden because you never need to open it.

## Where pictures and attachments go

Pictures, drawings, scans and recordings go in the folder named in Settings › Files and Links › **Default Location for New Attachments**. In this guide that is the folder *Attachments*.
